# Research methodology

Research snapshot: 11 October 2026.

This is a documented, selective research programme, not proof that every Catholic film has been discovered or every edition examined. The structured research inventory records candidate-level decisions and remaining gaps. The published selection must be read alongside that inventory; catalogue size is not a quality measure.

## Three evidentiary questions

**Identity:** establish the work, original and translated titles, first release year, director, country, language and format. Prefer national film archives, film institutes, production records and official distributors. Record territorial premieres, restored versions and television recuts instead of forcing conflicting dates into one unexplained value. An animated narrative is not a documentary.

**Religious treatment:** seek substantive criticism or detailed production evidence describing how the film presents belief, religious life and morally important choices. A synopsis, trailer, promotional quote or religious distributor's brand alone cannot establish fidelity. Published criticism may support a research-based editorial judgment; it must not be rewritten as the researcher's personal viewing experience.

**History and doctrine:** use primary historical documents, responsible historical scholarship, and applicable preconciliar doctrinal texts. Filmographic metadata cannot prove either accuracy or orthodoxy. Establish the historical claim independently from the film's portrayal. Attribute disputed readings and separate warranted inference from evidence.

## Candidate workflow

1. Preserve every supplied candidate, including uncertain spellings and dates, before normalization. Assign a stable identifier and link aliases to a single work. Log duplicates rather than silently losing candidates.
2. Establish identity; distinguish remakes, alternate cuts, miniseries and feature compilations. Do not populate missing fields with plausible guesses.
3. Record source title, publisher, exact URL, the date actually checked, and the claims or fields supported. Store relevant copyright or reuse restrictions separately from factual citations.
4. Read substantive treatment evidence, apply all eight questions in EDITORIAL_POLICY.md, and record both favorable evidence and material objections. Note whether an entire source, an excerpt or only search metadata was accessible. Discovery snippets do not count as a completed review.
5. Write original, film-specific prose. Quote only when necessary and within permitted limits. Do not copy a distributor's synopsis.
6. Assign PUBLISH, HOLD or REJECT with a concrete rationale. For HOLD, state the next evidence needed. For REJECT, distinguish outside scope from demonstrable incompatibility.
7. Run content validation and review the rendered profile, source list, advisories, related titles and collection membership. Commit the decision and its evidence together.

## Coverage and disclosure

Investigate silent cinema through 2026; features, television films, miniseries, documentaries and animation. The international scope includes Europe, the Americas, Asia and Africa. Query French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese and Polish titles and sources when useful. An English-language selection cannot substitute for national research.

The inventory should record which archives, catalogues and publications were actually examined, what was accessible, the candidate or claim examined and remaining questions. Do not imply an entire national catalogue has been exhaustively searched because one record was consulted. Source categories are a research strategy, not a claim that every named institution has been reviewed.

Particular gap checks include English and Welsh martyrs, the Vendée, Cristeros, Spanish Civil War martyrs, missionary history, lesser-known Marian productions, and films about Louis-Marie de Montfort, Jean-Marie Vianney, Pius X, Pio of Pietrelcina, Teresa of Ávila, John of the Cross and Catherine of Siena. Record absence of verified evidence rather than inventing a film to fill a collection.

## Source quality and conflicts

- A producer is authoritative for its own production information but interested in promotion. Seek independent interpretation.
- A national institute can establish film identity; it does not confer religious approval.
- A Catholic critic offers an attributable judgment, not automatically the Library's traditional standard.
- An audience-rating board supports specific content advisories; it does not establish Catholic fidelity.
- Crowd-edited databases and lists can suggest leads. Verify important claims against stronger sources.
- Two sites repeating the same press release are not independent corroboration.
- When sources disagree, record the precise discrepancy and the reason for preferring one. Leave material uncertainty on HOLD.
- A source URL's availability does not establish that every attached claim is supported. Review the actual text.

## Review dates and preservation

An access date records a source check, not a film viewing. An editorial date records the assessment of evidence then available, not guaranteed current distribution. Verify legitimate viewing links by territory before making availability claims. Prefer durable detail pages to generic homepages; preserve bibliographic information when a URL changes. External pages remain their owners' works and are not licensed for republication merely because they are cited.

Public metadata and the repository's JSON research records are exportable text. Keep permanent IDs and slugs stable. Version control supplies the correction history; record substantive factual and editorial changes in CHANGELOG.md. Recheck material disputed entries promptly and rotate older source checks without pretending the whole catalogue was reverified on every build.
