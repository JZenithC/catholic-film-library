# A–F research audit — 11 October 2026

## Scope and result

The JSON contains all 76 starting candidates assigned from sections A–F, plus three original-language discoveries: *Dieu a besoin des hommes*, *Le Défroqué* and *Gli uomini non guardano il cielo*. There are 24 PUBLISH and 55 HOLD decisions. HOLD is an evidence/workflow state, not a declaration that the film is anti-Catholic.

Every PUBLISH entry has a substantial original profile, at least two source records, verified country information, a stated rationale, historical/adaptation limits and content guidance. The profile evidence is published criticism and filmography. No claim of direct full-film viewing, frame-by-frame verification, current streaming entitlement or ecclesiastical certification is made.

## Evidence approach

AFI, BFI, national film archives, contemporary production/distributor material and repertory institutions establish identity. Detailed criticism establishes what the film does with its subject. Wikipedia, IMDb and aggregators are supplementary identity sources, not the sole basis for a fidelity decision. Unfetched pages were not treated as fully read: when a web search returned substantive indexed passages, only those passages informed the record.

Research consulted original French, Spanish and Italian material, not only English Catholic recommendation lists. Especially useful were Torino Film Festival's republication of the 1949 Centro Cattolico Cinematografico entry and other criticism for *Cielo sulla palude*; Instituto Cervantes' scene analysis of *Marcelino, pan y vino*; DVDClassik's close analysis of Bresson's Joan; Cathopedia's installment-specific sacramental scenes, quotations and edition differences for Don Camillo; and DeCine21's concrete discussion of *Fray Escoba*.

Decent Films criticism supplies strong scene-level religious evidence for several published classics and newer titles. Its evaluative vocabulary is not adopted wholesale. A positive rating, inclusion on the Vatican list, screening at Lourdes, Catholic distributor or saintly subject was never the sole reason for publication.

## Material distinctions retained

- *The Passion of the Christ*: the profile discloses that its cited Catholic analysis acknowledges legitimate criticism of the depiction of Jewish opponents. Recommendation rests on the documented redemptive and sacramental structure, not endorsement of every characterisation; collective guilt is expressly rejected.

- All PUBLISH decisions now state individualized reasons applying the editorial rubric. The 1965 Sound of Music uses the site's 'from 1965' collection (internal ID after-1965).

- *The Passion of Bernadette*: 1989 is used by Decent Films and MUBI; IMDb records French theatrical release on 9 May 1990. The profile states this, rather than silently treating different date conventions as different works. Original French is distinguished from English dubbing. The medical image includes an ulcerated breast, as CNS specifies.
- *Bernadette*: simultaneous French/English filming is documented. Production-country metadata includes France, Switzerland and Luxembourg according to IMDb; the country list is production information, not the story's setting.
- Don Camillo: only the 1952 and 1953 entries are published. The French and Italian versions differ; the French first film extends an attempted-suicide sequence. Later installments have their own HOLD records and are not automatically endorsed as a bundle.
- *The Sound of Music*: Maria is a postulant discerning a vocation. Her marriage is not represented as breaking solemn vows. The invented mountain escape and omitted historical priest-musical director are disclosed.
- *Marcelino*: a literary religious fable, not a purported canonically investigated miracle. The ending involves a child's death, requiring more parental context than its gentle surface suggests.
- *Heaven over the Marshes*: severe sexual violence and murder of a child make a mature-viewer recommendation essential. The profile does not imply that an assault victim's moral innocence depends on successful resistance.
- *Triumph of the Heart*: documented religious content includes confession, Marian prayer and identification with Christ's suffering. Private bunker dialogue and developed backstories remain reconstruction. It receives an adult advisory.
- *Mary of Nazareth*: producer evidence distinguishes a two-part miniseries from a 110-minute television-film cut. These must not be silently conflated.
- *La mies es mucha*: evaluated by the additions researcher; that batch owns the record to avoid a duplicate.

## Outstanding priorities

The richest follow-up prospects include La Señora de Fátima, the later Don Camillo installments, Quo Vadis, Becket, the Carmelite film, and the priesthood/family classics. Several currently have only identity or incomplete content coverage. These should receive full cut-specific review before promotion.

The detailed criticism surrounding A Man for All Seasons, Of Gods and Men, The Keys of the Kingdom, and some other candidates raises specific interpretive questions. Their HOLD records state the outstanding question without claiming that a dissenting critic's interpretation is settled fact.

For families, distinguish general accessibility from an exhaustive content certificate. Where no complete advisory inventory exists, the profile recommends parental preview instead of inventing a clean-content guarantee.

## Rights and deployment

All source links are references. No poster, frame, portrait, trailer, script, film file or other licensed artwork is supplied by this research. Source access does not imply permission to reproduce artwork, stream a film, or hold a public screening. Original profile prose is newly written; no source quotations are used in the profiles.

## Integration

Only PUBLISH entries belong in public film pages and navigation. HOLD entries belong in the internal audit export. Empty metadata fields on HOLD records are intentional. The data file and these notes do not claim a globally exhaustive catalogue or completion of every direct-viewing question.

All 24 published themes fields now contain original 73–79 word interpretive paragraphs, preserved as single-element arrays for import compatibility. La guerra de Dios was promoted using Cinematografo detailed narrative and contemporary Catholic criticism plus the Diocese of Cartagena social-doctrine review. An inaccessible Religion en Libertad link was omitted because the indexed extract was insufficiently attributable to this exact film. The IFI dead PDF was replaced with its working institutional retrospective page; the broken Bologna PDF was removed. Balarrasa remains HOLD after a fuller indexed scholarly conclusion exposed a specific interpretive disagreement, not merely a generic objection to its period.
