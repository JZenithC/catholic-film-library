# Modern, television, documentary and international research audit

Checked 11 October 2026. This file accompanies `research-modern.json`.

## Scope and outcome

All 20 candidates in G, 10 in H and 15 in I have an internal decision and a retrieved source. Nine additional discoveries were recorded. Final research-pass outcome: 54 records, 10 PUBLISH, 41 HOLD, 3 REJECT. The brief forbids certifying doctrine from a synopsis. Recommendations have independent scene-level evidence in addition to production or distributor material. Presence in an Ignatius catalogue is not used as ecclesiastical approval.

## Recommended entry

Into Great Silence has institutional identity evidence (BFI), production evidence (Ventura Film), detailed content evidence (Steven D. Greydanus), and a filmmaker interview. Its specific Christian content includes Scriptural intertitles and patristic Trinitarian chant. The profile describes a modern community without claiming that its every liturgical practice is pre-conciliar. Runtime varies by source/edition and is omitted. No film was watched in this research task.

## Corrections and hazards

- Jesus of Nazareth is not automatically acceptable because of reputation. Greydanus’s detailed review identifies a substantial softening of Judas’s responsibility and other Gospel departures. HOLD pending examination, rather than adopting either pre-release Protestant allegations or later praise wholesale.
- The Day the Sun Danced is animation. WorldCat OCLC 29191969 documents a 1991 copyrighted English VHS, not the proposed 1988 original release. Original release year remains unset.
- God’s Mighty Servant: the producer lists 2009, whereas the starting inventory lists 2011. These may be production and broadcast dates, but this distinction was not conclusively established. Year remains unset.
- The Blood & the Rose: a December 2012 premiere press release and a 2013 IMDb listing should not silently be collapsed. Year remains unset.
- Faustyna appeared at the 1994 Gdynia festival; English-language 1995 dates may correspond to theatrical distribution. The record uses the evidenced festival year.
- Padre Cícero was produced in 1975 and released at Gramado in January 1976, according to Cinemateca Brasileira. The record uses release year 1976.
- There are two distinct 2000 Padre Pio television productions. They must not be merged with each other, the 2022 Abel Ferrara film, or the 2024 Saint Pio docudrama.
- John Bosco: Mission to Love is distinct from Don Bosco (1988). Maria Goretti (2003) is distinct from Heaven over the Marshes (1949).
- Bakhita’s Sudanese subject does not make the film an African production.
- Ignatius’s Moscati page mixes film description with biography and contains a sweeping insulin-history claim; the claim was deliberately not reproduced.
- Ignatius’s Duns Scotus page places a debate generically in the 13th century. This distributor shorthand was not reproduced; fuller reviews and the doctrinal reference support the published profile's distinction between reconstructed debate and doctrine.
- Brother Sun, Sister Moon has meaningfully different Italian and international cuts according to ACEC Toscana. An edition-specific review is necessary.
- Au revoir les enfants is about rescue of Jewish children in a Catholic setting. HOLD concerns the exact catalogue scope and historical/film distinction, not the morality of rescue or an assumption of religious indifferentism.

## Source practice and limits

Every JSON source carries its exact supporting claims. Some entries intentionally cite a discovery lead (clearly identified as Wikipedia) rather than pretending archival verification. Sources retrieved through search results were used only for the visible claims. A search-indexed article can support its displayed passage when the direct page fails; such access limitations are identified in the newer records. No unaccessed film was treated as watched. Original profiles were written for the ten public entries; distributor copy was not reproduced. Sources are evidence links, not permissions to reproduce posters, stills, films or trailers.

Three REJECT decisions concern this library's requested scope: The Two Popes has documented historical misrepresentation and an explicit contemporary-reform framing; Conclave is interpreted by the cited mainstream review as a demand for doctrinal/institutional change; the shortened English Restless Heart fundamentally reconstructs the cause of Augustine's conversion. None claims an authoritative Church condemnation. The last judgment does not automatically extend to the complete Italian miniseries.

The regional programme is an evidenced sample, not a completed international census. African-produced cinema, Japanese religious-content assessment, Philippine final-release evidence, and detailed television screening remain significant work. Japanese archival identification of The Bells of Nagasaki was resolved in the final pass. The JSON gaps must stay visible in the overall research methodology and delivery report.

## Second-pass recommendations and resolved concerns

- **John Bosco: Mission to Love (2004): PUBLISH.** The Salesian Family blog republishes a detailed 2007 review in January 2026. It identifies papal obedience, catechism dialogue, and Savio's holiness rather than simply praising charity. It also specifies major chronological telescoping and composite opponents, now disclosed in the public profile. The editorial judgment is that the documented adaptation compresses institutional history without reversing the vocation's religious meaning.
- **Clare and Francis (2007): PUBLISH.** Greydanus supplies independent evidence of Church devotion and consecrated vocation. The full La Rocca report was accessible in a clearly labelled reproduction: Franciscan disagreement concerns artistic execution and depth. Positive responses explicitly distinguish spiritual friendship from invented romance. This is not represented as unanimous Franciscan approval.
- **Padre Pio: Miracle Man (2000): PUBLISH.** Greydanus's detailed scene criticism supports confession, vocation and Marian reverence. DVD Talk corroborates identity and dramatic structure. Mediaset supplies original April 2000 episode dates, correcting a typographical 1941 date in the Register review. Mediaset's promotional description of a priest rejecting authority was not adopted: a traditional Catholic historical commentary identifies the film's scene rebuking a mayor for attacking the bishop. The physical action is historically altered and is disclosed. The film is not confused with Base's 2000 production or Ferrara's later work.
- **Blessed Duns Scotus (2010): PUBLISH.** Independent English and Italian reviews give concrete evidence of Eucharistic contemplation, Marian argument, and the role of prayer in study. Papal loyalty is also documented. The profile distinguishes the novice-romance subplot and reconstructed debate from established theology. The 1854 definition in Ineffabilis Deus provides the pre-conciliar doctrinal reference, not a claimed film endorsement. Final audit corrected the earlier 2011 catalogue year: the primary Mirabile Dictu festival record identifies an Italian film of 2010, awarded in May 2011. The precise original public-release date remains unverified and is not inferred from its awards.

## Stronger HOLD evidence obtained

Saint Rita has an independent review flagging a potentially important vocational alteration in the marriage story. Moscati has a Linacre Quarterly review describing sacraments but also extensive invented romance and rivalry; inaccurate saint dates in that review were not adopted. Finding Fatima has diocesan review evidence of Eucharistic and Rosary themes, but its Third Secret interpretation still needs checking. A Chilean official cultural-programme report resolves the year and director of Teresa de los Andes (1989, Vicente Sabatini). Additional Blood and the Rose evidence reports a January 2013 Washington premiere and documents the film's historical-apparition connections; exact first-release chronology and those claims remain unresolved.

Further search found a Detroit Catholic review of Bakhita (2009) with specific mature-content advisories. More materially, Canossian specialist Maria Carla Frison disputes the fiction's portrayal of disobedient absence. This remains a substantive historical-characterization hold, not merely an unsearched candidate. Joseph of Nazareth also now has documented scene-level concerns beyond generic biblical invention. Finding Fatima and The Bells of Nagasaki, by contrast, remain principally evidence-limited; they are not asserted to teach error.

## Final-pass recommendations and boundaries

- **Saint Philip Neri (2010): PUBLISH.** An Oratorian superior's detailed assessment explicitly acknowledges extensive historical invention yet identifies prayer, confession, grace, humility and fidelity to ecclesiastical authority. The profile does not present the fictional children's Oratory as a literal history of the adult institution. This specialist evidence answers the earlier generic concern over loose biography.
- **Two Crowns (2017): PUBLISH.** Polish Catholic criticism describes Marian devotion, obedience to God's will and sacrificial charity. A less favorable film review checks inflated artistic claims: dramatic stiffness and repetitive testimony are not hidden. The profile does not adopt every public controversy about Kolbe as a settled historical finding.
- **No Greater Love (2009): PUBLISH.** BFI independently verifies year, country and director. A filmmaker interview and detailed reviews establish an explicitly Christian year of prayer, profession, death and Easter. The profile now specifically discloses the prioress's speculation that atheists might be right and nothing follows death, rather than subsuming it under generic dryness. The contextual adult recommendation rests on the documentary's sustained profession and Resurrection structure, not approval of that statement as doctrine. The contemporary monastery's later customs are identified; it is not offered as a reconstruction of pre-conciliar discipline or an uncomplicated catechetical model.
- **Teresa de Jesús (1984): PUBLISH.** E. T. Gil de Muro's specialist Diccionario teresiano entry assesses fidelity to Teresa's writings and interior journey, while separate Spanish Catholic criticism assesses the fair portrayal of ecclesiastical scrutiny. The earlier scholarly medical reading of bodily ecstasy is not evidence that the film itself denies grace. Limitations remain disclosed without transforming a critic's interpretation into film doctrine.
- **All That Remains (2016): PUBLISH.** Additional British discovery on the Japanese Nagai family. Festival identity, distributor outline, scene-specific Catholic criticism of the ending and the director's survivor-research testimony establish the basis. The final reunion is dramatic imagery, not an apparition report or canonization. It must not fill the Japanese-production coverage gap by proxy.

The strongest remaining titles received repeated English and original-language searches. Further catalogue growth still requires better evidence, especially complete versions or transcripts for unresolved scenes, rather than changing the threshold to reach a number.
