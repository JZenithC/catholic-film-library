# Editorial policy

Policy date: 11 October 2026 (revised in the editorial pass of that date). Final editorial authority: the editor.

## The standard

The Catholic Film Library does not accept the Second Vatican Council or the reforms that followed it. It judges films by Catholic doctrine and moral teaching as the Church taught them before the Council. The site states this position publicly on its "How films are chosen" page.

The standard is not a cut-off date. A film made in 2004 can meet it, and a film made in 1950 can fail it. A modern date neither qualifies nor disqualifies a film.

### Exclusion rules (editorial rulings, 2026-10-11)

Two rules follow from the standard. Each excludes a film outright, however good it is otherwise:

- **(a) Saints.** A film is excluded if its subject is recognised as a saint or blessed only through a canonisation or beatification made after 1965.
- **(b) The post-conciliar Church.** A film is excluded if its Catholic content is the post-conciliar Church presented as normal or exemplary: the new liturgy, religious life as reformed after the Council, or interreligious dialogue.

A record excluded under one of these rules is REJECT, and its decision begins `REJECT: Scope (editorial ruling, 2026-10-11)` and says which rule applies. Where the editor applies the editorial position to a case the two rules do not settle on their own terms, the decision says `Editor ruling (2026-10-11)`. The editor can overturn any such ruling.

## Scope

Only Catholic-centred films are included (editorial decision). The faith has to be the subject: the Mass and the sacraments, the life of a saint, a priest's or a religious vocation, Our Lady and her apparitions, or a persecution of the Church. A sympathetic priest in a supporting role, a church wedding or a generally spiritual mood is not enough. On this ground The Sound of Music and On the Waterfront were removed from the site.

## Evidence and authority

A recommendation is an editorial judgment, not an imprimatur, a theological certification or a substitute for parental judgment. Do not describe a film as Church approved, guaranteed orthodox or free of controversy. An award, a religious distributor, a famous director or a saintly subject does not by itself establish that a film is sound.

**Pre-conciliar reference texts.** These shape the standard. None of them approves any particular film.

- Pius XI, [Vigilanti cura](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xi/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-xi_enc_29061936_vigilanti-cura.html), 1936: the moral influence of cinema and the duty of audiences and critics.
- Pius XI, [Mortalium animos](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xi/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-xi_enc_19280106_mortalium-animos.html), 1928: religious unity; the measure for films that treat other religions.
- Pius XII, [Mediator Dei](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-xii_enc_20111947_mediator-dei.html), 1947: the liturgy and the Eucharistic sacrifice; relevant when a film shows the Mass or the priesthood.
- Pius XII, [Miranda prorsus](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-xii_enc_08091957_miranda-prorsus.html), 1957: film, radio and television, and responsible film criticism.
- The [Roman Catechism](https://archive.org/details/catechismofcounc00cath) (Catechism of the Council of Trent), 1566: the Passion and the sacraments.
- Council of Trent, [Session XXV](https://history.hanover.edu/texts/trent/ct25.html), 1563: the saints, relics and sacred images. Applying it to cinema is an analogy; the decree is not a film-rating system.

When one of these texts is used to assess a film, find the specific passage. A link to a whole encyclical does not prove a claim about a scene.

**Pre-1965 Catholic film ratings.** Ratings and reviews by the Legion of Decency and other Catholic film offices before 1965 (for example the Italian Centro Cattolico Cinematografico or the French Centrale Catholique) are legitimate evidence about how a film was judged under the old standard.

**Post-conciliar bodies and publications.** CNS and USCCB ratings, Vatican statements after 1965, the 1992 Catechism, the 1995 Vatican film list and similar material may be cited for facts (identity, content, release history). They are never cited as approving evidence. A film whose only favourable reviews come from post-conciliar Catholic outlets cannot be recommended on that basis. It is GREY (not yet verified) at most.

Independent critics, film archives and national film institutes are used for identity, content and craft. They do not confer religious approval.

## Eight questions for every film

Record a reasoned, sourced answer to each question that applies. Do not turn the answers into a score.

1. **Catholic substance.** Is the faith central, or only the setting?
2. **Doctrine.** Does the film's own outlook fit traditional teaching? A character's error is not the film's endorsement. Check major claims against a relevant pre-conciliar text.
3. **Reverence.** How does it treat Our Lord, Our Lady, the saints, the sacraments, priests and religious? Humour or a flawed priest is not irreverent in itself, and a religious setting does not excuse ridicule of sacred things.
4. **History.** What is documented and what is invented, and do the inventions change the religious meaning? Fiction need not be a documentary.
5. **Craft.** What is good about the acting, images, writing, editing or music? Reputation alone does not count.
6. **Reinterpretation.** Does it reduce the supernatural to psychology, treat all religions as interchangeable, or recast doctrine? This has to be shown from evidence about the film, not assumed from who made it or when.
7. **Controversy.** Is there a documented dispute about its religion or history? Describe the objection and its evidence fairly.
8. **Audience.** Who is it for? Record violence, sexual content, frightening scenes and age-sensitive material separately. A film suited to adults is not assumed to suit children.

## Outcomes

There are three public outcomes, plus two that are not published.

**PUBLISH (recommended).** This needs a verified identity and substantive evidence about how the film treats the faith, beyond a promotional synopsis. It also needs original editorial text, a satisfactory answer to the doctrinal and historical questions, advisories where the evidence supports them, sources with the claims each supports, and a recorded reason for the decision. Prefer independent sources. Distributor enthusiasm alone cannot pass this gate. Omit missing optional facts; do not invent them.

**GREY (grey area).** The film is Catholic-centred and has real merit, but it carries an open question. It gets a full film page that opens with a box stating the question. The record carries `grey: {kind, question}`, and the decision text begins `GREY:`. There are two kinds:

- `reservations`: a real doctrinal, moral or historical question, such as a Legion of Decency reservation, an invented episode that changes the religious meaning, or an ambiguous treatment of a priest's failure;
- `unverified`: the film looks sound, but no independent review of how it treats the faith or the history has been found.

The question must say what evidence would settle it. Grey films are kept out of the default library view, featured films, Start here and the homepage. They can be found through search (with the grey-area toggle), the A–Z page, collection sub-lists and `/grey-area/`. A grey film can never be featured. `scripts/schema.mjs` and `scripts/validate.mjs` enforce these rules, including a 40–120-word question.

**HOLD.** There is not enough reliable evidence to place the film: an unresolved identity, competing cuts, no access to the sources, or no evidence about its treatment. Record what evidence would resolve it. Lack of evidence is not a condemnation. Appearing in the starting inventory is not approval.

**REJECT.** This needs a documented reason: one of the two exclusion rules, the scope rule, or a demonstrated problem too grave to leave to the viewer. Rejecting a film from the catalogue does not assert that nobody may watch it. An unverified suspicion belongs on HOLD.

Only PUBLISH and GREY records reach the site, search, related-film links, structured data and `catalogue.json`. HOLD and REJECT records stay in `research/inventory.json` as research records and are never published; `scripts/check-leak.mjs` checks the build for them.

## Writing

Write in a plain critic's voice. Say what the film is, why it is worth watching, what it does with the faith and the history, and what to know before watching it. Film pages use the headings "Why watch it" ("Why it is worth knowing about" on a grey page), "The faith in the film", "The history", "What the film changes" and "Before you watch".

Avoid talk about "the Library", repeated disclaimers and filler piety. The style lint in `scripts/schema.mjs` flags the worst phrases as warnings.

## Working rules

- State whether a judgment rests on published criticism, archive records, production material or an actual viewing. Never claim or imply a viewing that did not happen. No film was watched for the current catalogue.
- Separate what the film shows, what happened historically and what the editor concludes. Attribute critics' readings to the critics.
- Approval of an apparition does not verify a film's dialogue. Private revelation is not public revelation.
- Describe persecution and martyrdom accurately without blaming whole present-day peoples or religions for what historical actors did.
- Check every saint's or blessed's date of canonisation or beatification against rule (a).
- Do not read a source's silence as evidence that a film has no objectionable content. A classification board's rating is evidence about content, not about doctrine.
- Never let a desired count, a collection gap, the design or a deadline override these gates. An empty collection carries a note explaining the gap.

## Review and corrections

Keep the decision, evidence, open questions, review date and changes in version control. New evidence can move any film in either direction. Withdraw a public recommendation while a material concern is being resolved, keep the record's identifier, and redirect a removed film URL to `/library/` (see `astro.config.mjs`). Open verification items are listed in each record's decision and in `research/editorial-notes.json`. Disputed decisions rest with the editor. Routine work within this policy does not need repeated approval.
