# Editorial policy

Policy date: 11 October 2026. Final editorial authority: the Founder.

The Catholic Film Library is an independent reference project guided by Catholic doctrine and moral teaching as understood before the Second Vatican Council. Its purpose is to help readers discover worthwhile cinema, not to adjudicate contemporary ecclesiastical disputes. A release after 1965 is not grounds for exclusion. A release before 1965 is not evidence of fidelity.

## Authority and evidence

A recommendation is the Library's fallible editorial judgment, not an imprimatur, a theological certification, or a substitute for parental discretion. Do not describe a film as Church approved, guaranteed orthodox, or free of controversy without specific verifiable authority applicable to that film and edition. An award, religious distributor, famous director, or saintly subject cannot establish doctrinal compatibility by itself.

The following primary texts inform the principles, rather than approve any title:

- Pius XI, [Vigilanti cura](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xi/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-xi_enc_29061936_vigilanti-cura.html), 29 June 1936: cinema's moral influence and the responsibility of audiences and critics.
- Pius XII, [Miranda prorsus](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-xii_enc_08091957_miranda-prorsus.html), 8 September 1957: discernment of entertainment and responsible film criticism.
- Pius XII, [Mediator Dei](https://www.vatican.va/content/pius-xii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-xii_enc_20111947_mediator-dei.html), 20 November 1947: the sacrificial and sacramental character of the Eucharist; relevant when a film interprets priesthood or the Mass.
- Council of Trent, [Session XXV](https://history.hanover.edu/texts/trent/ct25.html), 1563, decree on invocation, veneration, relics and sacred images, in the Hanover Historical Texts Project's English transcription: sacred representation, saints' intercession, and the exclusion of misleading religious imagery. Application to cinema is the Library's analogy; the decree is not a film-rating system.

These online texts were checked on 11 October 2026. Locate the specific passage when using one to assess a film; a link to a whole encyclical does not prove a scene-level claim. Respect differences between doctrinal propositions, disciplines, historical circumstances, and prudential judgments.

## Eight questions for every film

Record a reasoned answer, supported by sources, to each applicable question. Do not convert answers into an orthodoxy score.

1. **Catholic substance:** Is Catholic belief, life, history, worship or witness central enough to warrant inclusion? Generic spirituality and incidental Catholic characters are insufficient.
2. **Doctrinal compatibility:** Does the film's framing support, contradict, or leave uncertain relevant traditional teaching? Distinguish a character's error from the work's apparent endorsement. Check major claims against a pertinent primary text.
3. **Reverence:** How are Our Lord, Our Lady, saints, sacraments, priesthood and religious life represented? Humour or a flawed priest is not automatically irreverence; a religious setting does not excuse ridicule of sacred realities.
4. **Historical integrity:** Which people and events are documented, and which scenes, composites, chronology or dialogue are dramatized? Assess whether a change distorts the religious meaning, rather than demanding documentary literalism from fiction.
5. **Artistic value:** What specific achievement in performance, composition, writing, editing, music or observation makes the work worth recommending? Prestige alone is insufficient.
6. **Doctrinal reinterpretation:** Does the work endorse religious indifferentism, replace supernatural belief with an exclusively psychological account, or reinterpret Catholic doctrine in a materially incompatible way? Substantiate the concern; do not infer it from a filmmaker's identity or release date.
7. **Material controversy:** What documented disagreement concerns this film's religious or historical representation? Describe the objection and its evidence fairly, rather than treating controversy itself as proof of error.
8. **Recommendation and audience:** Could a cautious traditional Catholic audience reasonably be recommended this particular edition without a substantial unresolved doctrinal reservation? Record violence, sexual material, disturbing situations and age-sensitive content separately. Adult suitability is not family suitability.

## Decision gates

**PUBLISH** requires verified identity, substantive evidence about treatment beyond a promotional synopsis, original editorial text, sufficient resolution of relevant doctrinal and historical concerns, explicit advisories where supported, claim-level source records, and a recorded positive rationale. Prefer independent sources for identity and interpretation. A carefully documented substantive critical source may cover several questions, but distributor enthusiasm alone cannot pass the gate. Missing nonessential runtime or cast data should be omitted, not invented.

**HOLD** is the default for a candidate with unresolved identity, inadequate treatment evidence, competing cuts, disputed interpretation, inaccessible sources, or an unresolved substantial reservation. Record what exact evidence would resolve the uncertainty. Lack of evidence is not a condemnation. A candidate's presence in the Founder's starting inventory is not approval.

**REJECT** requires a documented scope or substantive incompatibility reason, supported by references when making a factual or interpretive claim. Rejecting inclusion does not assert that every viewer is forbidden to watch the film. Unverified suspicions belong on HOLD.

Only PUBLISH records enter recommendations, film routes, public search, related-film links, structured data and public catalogue exports. Internal HOLD/REJECT records may remain in the public source repository as research records; they must be clearly labelled as nonrecommendations and not masquerade as film profiles.

## Working rules

- State whether research is based on published criticism, archival metadata, production material, or an actual viewing. Never claim or imply a viewing that did not occur. Identify the cut and language when a viewing is recorded.
- Separate film portrayal, historical event and editorial interpretation. Attribute critics' interpretations. Never present a reconstructed scene as a verified event.
- Approval of an apparition does not verify a film's dialogue or endorse a particular dramatic interpretation. Do not make private revelation equivalent to public revelation.
- Describe persecution and martyrdom accurately without blaming whole present-day populations or religions for historical actors' conduct.
- Treat titles involving later canonizations with care: identify the film and historical person accurately; investigate rather than silently assuming the Founder accepts every later ecclesiastical designation or interpretation.
- Do not infer absence of objectionable content from a source's silence. A classification is useful audience evidence, not a doctrinal review.
- Never let a desired count, collection gap, visual design or deadline override the gates. Empty collections should explain a research gap.

## Review and corrections

Keep the decision, evidence, unresolved questions, review date and changes in version control. New credible evidence can move any title back to HOLD. Remove its public recommendation while resolving a material concern; preserve its identifier and an appropriate neutral withdrawn-page or redirect strategy where practical. Final disputed decisions rest with the Founder. Routine evidence-based implementation within this policy does not require repeated approval.
